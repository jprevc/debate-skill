This Report was produced by a fixed procedure for structured debate. Every role in the debate (Advocates, Auditors and the Judge) was performed by AI language models (Claude). Every step is recorded in files, so each Claim can be traced back to its source document.

**Advocates.** <For each group of Dimensions, / For the Motion,> there were two Advocates, one for Pro and one for Con. Every round was carried out by a new, independent agent that knew only the Motion and the debate record so far. It knew nothing about the views of the person who commissioned the debate. Every Claim is labelled with its type (Factual, Inference, Predictive or Value). A Factual Claim must cite Evidence. Evidence is a verbatim quote from a source, stored in its own file together with a copy of the source document.

**Course of the debate.**

1. **Opening Case.** Each Side presents its Claims.
2. **Rebuttal.** Each Side answers every Claim of the other Side: it disputes it, limits its significance or concedes it.
3. **Evidence Audit.** Independent Auditors reopened every piece of Evidence in its source<, in <N> batches>. They checked whether the quote exists, whether it is taken out of context and whether it supports the Claims that cite it. They assigned each source a tier.
4. **Rejoinder.** Each Side answers the other Side's Rebuttal and may correct or withdraw its own Claims.
5. **Rejoinder Audit.** Evidence added in the Rejoinder was checked the same way.
6. **Judgment.** The Judge read the whole record and wrote this Report.

**Evidence Requests.** When an Advocate could not obtain a document, it filed an Evidence Request. Some could be met later. The Report treats Claims that rest on unmet requests as unsupported.

**What this Report is and is not.** The Report is a map of the disputed questions. It does not recommend a decision.

- **Factual Cruxes** are disagreements that Evidence could settle. They are marked settled, leaning towards one Side, or unsettled.
- **Value Cruxes** remain even when the Sides agree on the facts. Only the reader can decide them, by their own priorities.

Every factual statement in the Report links to Evidence that the Audit did not reject. Where the Auditor found a limit (the quote supports a Claim only in part, or its context narrows it), the Evidence window shows it as a partial result.

**Limitations.**

- The Audit checks whether a quote is accurate and correctly used, not whether the source itself is right.
- A Source Tier says how reliable a source is, not which Side is right.
- The number of Claims and Evidence for each Side is not a score of who won.
- The debate used only sources that were publicly available up to <the date of the last Stage> or supplied by the person who commissioned it.
- Language models can make mistakes. That is why every quote links to its source, so readers can check it themselves.
